# What the literature already establishes

Search date: 26 November 2026. Repository maintainer: Yingbo Sun (orcarider7).

## Claims to compare

1. In a one-dimensional periodic chain of alternating charges $m,-1$, use pair energy $-q_iq_j/r^p$, $p>1$, at fixed positive mean gap. Equal spacing is the unique minimum up to translation for every density and every even period exactly when $m+m^{-1}\leq2^{p+1}-2$. Above that threshold the energy is unbounded below for all sufficiently large even periods. The distinction from a known sufficient bound can be tested at $p=2,m=5$. [Model, proof and equality cases](README.md)
2. At $p=2,m=6,N=78$, the equidistant state is a strict local minimum modulo translation, yet compressing a certified configuration makes the energy tend to $-\infty$ at the same period and density. Period 96 is the first even period with a negative direction in the uniform-state Hessian. [Finite-period Hessian and upper witness](inverse-square.md)
3. At these same parameters, the first collapsing even period is exactly 78. An inequality checked on a 75-site open block proves a minimum exists for every even period through 76. [Finite-period cutoff and integer certificate](collapse-onset.md)

For the exact period, a further search covered variants of “first collapsing period”, “finite cell”, “inverse square”, “signed inverse-power”, “non-neutral alternating chains”, “charge ratio” and “78”. The 2020 paper uses Jensen inequalities on gaps and explicitly studies a sufficient crystallization condition. Its Corollary 2.3 does not reach $p=2,m=6$. The [Calogero model](https://iris.uniroma1.it/handle/11573/12567) and [multispecies Calogero model](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0307126) feature inverse-square terms in different quantum Hamiltonians. We found no statement of the 78-site cutoff for this classical sign convention in the sources checked. Convexity, integer certification and network flows themselves are established techniques.

An older theorem expressed in different variables could imply a result without citing the 2020 article. The [original public version](https://github.com/orcarider7/alternating-riesz-chains/tree/2d31562a589b24e7557a0740c28e676d3db23da8), [previous finite-cell results](https://github.com/orcarider7/alternating-riesz-chains/tree/v0.1.0) and [exact cutoff release](https://github.com/orcarider7/alternating-riesz-chains/tree/v0.2.0) distinguish the chronology of this project. A reference establishing an equivalent result would change the priority assessment. Independent human and journal review remain outstanding.
